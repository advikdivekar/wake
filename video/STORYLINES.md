# Storylines: The Last Satellite, product demo

Reference: the anmoldoesmotion "OpenAI" concept reel (1920×1080, ~30 s). Its arc runs from dark chaos ("too many signals / tabs / thoughts") to lines that converge, branch into labelled nodes and resolve in a ring ("connected."). That zooms into a bright product world, then the prompt, the output burst, one word ("Seconds."), a code editor with checks and one red failure, a branching tree, the claim slot-roll and the mark.

## A. Too many signals (recommended, and built)

*Device:* the feature as the hook (real telemetry drowning the viewer, then one signal pulled out of it). *Form:* object-to-object chain: a trace becomes a waveform, the waveform becomes a ring, the ring opens into the live product.

The opening is every OPS-SAT channel at once: real telemetry, too many lines to read. One segment (#204) is pulled out, measured, cited and scored; the ring fills to TabPFN's 1.000 and opens onto the real console. Then the live console proves it: hide the label, reveal the truth, show the one it missed, rank 529 segments, beat the published benchmark, fine-tune for $0.83 (and keep the regression), and ask the archive. The archive says what it does not know.

- First 2 s: nine real channel traces scrolling across the void; "2,123 signals" lands with a marigold highlight block.
- Beats: hook (signal chaos) → tension (one segment, no label) → reveal (ring 1.000 → the console's title) → proof (hide/reveal, the miss, the queue, 0.996, the lab, Tinker, the archive) → claim (measured / cited / not known) → end card.
- Look: the console's own: void #050507, candle ink, a marigold accent, Fraunces display, JetBrains Mono labels; real screen recordings at 1.2–2.2×.
- Sound: a dark minimal pulse for the chaos; the bed opens on the ring zoom-through; a bass hit on 0.996 and on the mark.

## B. The last transmission

*Device:* a moment in someone's day (an operator on 22 May 2024, the last pass). *Form:* one continuous take, from the timeline's last entry down into the archive. Quieter and more elegiac; it leans on the Elegy timeline and the finale. It is weaker on the proof, because the product's verbs come late.

## C. Read twice (bold)

*Device:* the object is the explanation. *Form:* split screen to one. The left half is TabPFN reading telemetry; the right half is the Tinker interpreter reading documents. They play in parallel until both land on the same investigation card (measured on one side, cited on the other) and the panes collapse into it. It is striking, but it is the hardest to read at phone size.
